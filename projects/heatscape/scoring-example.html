01

One slider per indicator

There is no category multiplier. Each of the 43 indicators has a single control holding its Shapley value — the share of explained variance in heat-emergency incidence the decomposition attributes to it — and everything the app scores is built from those 43 numbers.

A category has no weight of its own. It is worth the sum of the sliders inside it, and it reads as the plain mean of the indicators still switched on. Weighting happens once, between categories; inside a category every live indicator counts the same. Human co‑location, for instance, contributes

0.350191 × ( 99.8 + 98.5 ) / 2 = 34.7214  pedestrian flow 0.214982 + points of interest 0.135209 0.229475 × 99.8 = 22.9016  built environment — one indicator, so the mean is the reading

The priority score is the straight sum of those nine terms, gated by population. Shapley shares add to 1 across the full indicator set, so a cell that can be read everywhere lands on 0–100 with no normalising constant — and a cell with gaps scores lower, because there is less evidence of risk in it rather than because the missing categories were quietly redistributed.

priority = Σcategories ( Σ φ over live indicators × mean of their directed percentiles ) × gate   live = a share above zero and a reading in this cell
Zero means gone, not muted

Pulling a slider to zero removes that indicator from its category’s mean as well as from its weight. Eighteen indicators arrive at zero already — the decomposition gave them nothing — so canopy cover, albedo, drinking-fountain distance and fifteen others take no part in the score at all. That is also why permeable paving was dropped as an intervention: its levers were impervious ground and canopy cover, and neither carries attribution.

02

The priority score, step by step

Step 1 — turn each reading into a directed percentile

Every indicator is ranked against all 16,525 cells in the city. If the indicator protects against heat, the percentile is flipped so that high always means worse. This cell has 14 street trees, the 7th percentile citywide; trees protect, so it enters at 93.0. Pedestrian flow of 1,019 ped/m is the 99.8th percentile and enters as it is.

Step 2 — average the live indicators in each category

A plain mean, over the indicators that both carry a share and have a reading here. Human co‑location has two, both near the top of the city, and both count the same even though footfall is attributed 1.6× what points of interest are — that difference is spent on the category’s weight, not inside it.

mean = (99.8 + 98.5) / 2 = 99.15   Σφ = 0.350191

Adaptation capacity shows what the plain mean changes. It carries almost as much attribution as built volume, and this cell scores low on it: 97.4% of households here have air conditioning, the 93rd percentile citywide and so a directed value of 6.7. Under the old within-category weighting that one indicator held four fifths of the category and dragged it to 19.4. Now the three live indicators average evenly to 33.0 — distance to a cooling centre gets a real say.

mean = (6.7 + 71.6 + 20.7) / 3 = 33.00   Σφ = 0.228576

Step 3 — weight each category by its summed share and add up

Behaviour & mental health has no readings in this cell at all, so it contributes nothing and nothing is renormalised to cover for it. Note the small shortfalls elsewhere: social determinants is worth 0.043197 on paper but 0.043123 here, because utility insecurity has no reading and drops out of the sum along with the mean.

Step 4 — gate on residents

Interventions are not prioritised where nobody lives, so the score is scaled by min(1, pop / 50). With 920 residents this cell is fully gated in and the multiplier is 1.

75.7103 × 1 = 75.71  → displayed as 76

That puts the cell 362nd of 9,486 populated cells, inside the top 3.8%. For scale, the highest-scoring cell in the city reaches 85.5 and the median populated cell sits at 48.6 — the ceiling is below 100 because no cell is at the top of every category at once, and because 18 indicators are switched off citywide.

03

The top intervention

Interventions read the same sliders. Each one names the levers it acts on, weights them by their current Shapley values, and renormalises over its own lever list — so re‑weighting an indicator moves the intervention ranking along with the map.

36.166 / 0.393989 = 91.79 × gate 1 = 91.8 / 100

Building shadow has no reading in this cell, so it drops out and the remaining three renormalise — which is why the divisor is 0.393989 rather than the intervention’s full lever weight. The cell is at the 99.8th percentile for footfall and the 98.5th for points of interest, and those two carry 89% of the live weight between them. Summer surface temperature pulls the other way at 31.8, and it is the only thing keeping the score off the high nineties.

Cool roofs comes in 2.9 points behind on almost the same evidence: built volume is 99.8 here, but with only two live levers the same lukewarm temperature reading counts for 16% of its score instead of 11%. Plant street trees lands third — it is now 77% pedestrian flow, so it tracks the corridors closely and separates only on the tree count.

New open & green space sits well down the list at 48.3. Its social‑determinants lever is a category factor: it takes that category’s summed weight (0.043123) and its plain mean (60.2), which is exactly what would happen if all six live social indicators were listed as levers individually. Under the old weighting that lever read 73.2 because housing insecurity dominated it; the plain mean brings crowding at 16.7 and children at 2.0 back into view and pulls it down.

Indoor cooling support finishes last at 20.3, for the same reason adaptation capacity scored low in step 2: near‑universal air conditioning. That is the ranking doing its job — this block does not need subsidised AC, it needs shade over the sidewalk.

What this worksheet does not say

  • A Shapley share is unsigned. It says how much of the model’s fit an indicator accounts for, never in which direction it pushes. Direction comes from the systems diagram and no slider changes it.
  • The decomposition rests on 6 point fits and 16 bootstrap replicates. Shares under about 1% are not separable from one another, and most per‑indicator intervals span zero. Read the top of the table, not the tail.
  • Eighteen of the 43 indicators receive no attribution at all. That is what the decomposition found at 250 m — most of them arrive as tract‑level rates and barely vary between cells inside a tract — not a judgement that they are causally irrelevant.
  • The Equal preset gives every indicator the same share, which makes a category worth its size. Physiological vulnerability and social determinants hold 23 of the 43 indicators between them, so that map leans hard on tract-level health and census rates and looks very unlike this one. The gap between them is the attribution’s whole effect.
